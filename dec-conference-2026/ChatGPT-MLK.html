STOP 01 · THE GATHERING

A crowd with a purpose

On August 28, 1963, about 250,000 people came to Washington, D.C., for the March on Washington for Jobs and Freedom. They called for racial equality, fair employment, and civil rights.

Did you know?

People traveled by bus, train, car, and plane from across the country to take part.

✦   STOP & THINK

Why might gathering peacefully in such a large group attract national attention?

A large, peaceful gathering can show leaders that many people want change—and help the public hear their message.

A guide to your experience

How to explore

This interactive illustration is inspired by the National Mall in 1963. It helps you imagine the location and scale of the March on Washington; it does not replace historical photographs or primary sources.

Navigate: Drag on the scene (or swipe on a touch screen) to look around. Use the five stops, Next, or Previous to move to a new viewpoint. You can also click landmark labels.

Keyboard: Left/right arrows change stops; A/D look left or right; R resets the view; Escape exits a popup.

Learn: Read each stop and try the reflection question. Finish all five stops to unlock a short knowledge check.
